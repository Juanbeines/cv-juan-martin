    03 How we work

    Your five principles, and where I've lived them.

    Pencil's post describes how the optimisation practice works. I read it as values, because that's what they are. Each one has a story behind it.

    01 real briefs

    Start from the client's real briefs

    Agree what good looks like in terms precise enough to measure.

    My favorite place to work is the customer's office, sitting with the people who do the job and mapping it step by step. Their real artifacts beat any spec I could write from a distance.

    On-site discovery · US enterprise clients today
    02 baseline

    Measure before changing anything

    Every improvement shown against a baseline.

    As an AWS Well-Architected Review Consultant I walked customers through their workloads pillar by pillar before recommending a single change. A fix without a before is just an opinion.

    AWS Well-Architected Review Consultant
    03 one variable

    Change one thing at a time

    Keep a record of what changed and why.

    My production agent routes across models and falls back across providers. With that many moving parts, the only way to know what helped is one change, one note, one measurement.

    Production agent · model routing + fallbacks
    04 diagnose

    Treat a failure as a diagnosis problem

    The cause may sit in the inputs, the instructions or the measurement.

    Years of ETL and ML pipelines on AWS for banks and retailers taught me that most "model problems" start upstream, in the data or in how we measured. I check the inputs and the ruler before I touch the agent.

    Data lakes, ETL & ML pipelines · BGH
    05 repeat

    Done only when it holds up on repeat

    Then it's worth generalising.

    I say "not feasible" when it isn't. A result that only works on the demo day is a churned customer that hasn't happened yet. I'd rather lose the argument on day 3 than the account on day 90.

    Trusted advisor through POCs · DevRev
